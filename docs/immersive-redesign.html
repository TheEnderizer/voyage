Voyage · Immersive theme · design proposal

The cover
is the room.

Immersive paints a book cover across the whole app, then covers it with a 78%-opaque sheet of theme background. The artwork is wallpaper. This proposal makes it the subject — and rebuilds Home, the player and the mini bar around that one rule.

What's actually there now

Immersive is Material You wearing a translucent coat

Not a slight — it's literally the architecture. ui/immersive/home/HomeScreen.kt is a 31-line wrapper that hands ImmersiveHomeStyle to the same HomeScreenContent Material uses. The theme swaps colours; it never changes what is on screen or how big it is. Six things follow from that.

Findings
01 Backdrop
The cover is 22% visible

AppBlurredBackdrop paints the baked cover, then a flat background sheet at alpha 0.78 over the entire screen. Whatever the artwork was, it arrives as grey-blue fog.

02 Top of Home
The first 150dp show no art at all

A 26sp “Library”, a count and four filled chips occupy the top fifth of the screen before a single cover appears. That real estate is where a cover-first theme should be loudest.

03 Cards
Six drawn objects per cover

Background plate, 2.5dp accent border, badge pill, black-55% play disc, 82%-black scrim band, progress bar — all competing with the art on a 130dp-wide tile.

04 Player
Seven stacked bands below the cover

Chapter pill, title, author, chapter slider, time row, book bar, second time row, transport, secondary icons. Two sliders and two time rows say the same thing twice.

05 Mini bar
The cover is the smallest element

A 48dp thumbnail inset in a 64dp pill, with a straight 2dp progress line drawn under a fully rounded shape — and text in onSurface, the one place Immersive is not accent-tinted.

06 Glass
Cover-sampled, then neutralised

GlassPillSurface does real work smudging the cover — then flattens it under Color.Black at 0.30, so every pill lands the same grey regardless of the book.

The rule

At any moment, the largest and brightest thing on screen is cover art

Everything else is glass over it or type on it. Three laws follow, and they settle every question below without further debate.

LAW 01

Art at full strength, legibility from shape

The flat dim sheet goes. Contrast comes from gradients shaped to the content that needs them — a scrim under text, nothing under empty space. One vertical gradient replaces the 0.78 wall: light at the top where art lives, heavier at the bottom where the grid is dense.

LAW 02

No plates, only glass or nothing

If a control needs a ground it gets the cover-sampled pill. If it does not, it sits on a gradient with no container at all. Filled chips, card background plates and opaque black discs are all replaced under this law.

LAW 03

Colour is borrowed, never chosen

Accent already derives from the cover. Extend that to the glass tint and the scrim base: darken toward lerp(Black, primary, 0.12) instead of pure black, so a warm book gets warm glass and a cold one gets cold glass.

Changes
Hero
A sharp window into the backdrop

250dp of the now-playing cover at full strength, top-anchored so it runs under the status bar, resolving out of the blur behind it. Series eyebrow, title, progress hairline, Resume pill, time left.

It parallaxes at 0.5× as the grid scrolls over it and cross-fades into the blurred backdrop — the hero literally becomes the wallpaper. Past ~70% scrolled the title docks into a small glass header pill, so context is never lost.

Headline
“Library / 11 books” is deleted

The count moves into the tab row as superscripts; sort moves onto the hero as an unfilled glyph. Nothing announces the screen you are already looking at.

Tabs
Chips become a tracked text row

Four filled rectangles across the artwork become uppercase 9sp labels with an accent underline on the active one. Law 02.

Cards
Cover-only tiles

The card background plate goes — the art fills the tile. The scrim starts at 56% height with three stops instead of a hard 44% band, so a third more artwork survives.

Play disc: black-55% → cover-sampled glass with a hairline edge. The author line drops to 7.5sp uppercase and tracked, so the title owns the tile.

Now playing
One signal, not three

The 2.5dp border, the badge pill and the inset progress bar collapse into a single accent hairline welded to the card's bottom edge — which is the progress bar — plus a small waveform glyph before the title.

Bottom
Mini bar and nav pill fuse

Two floating pills stacked 12dp apart become one 26dp-radius glass slab with a hairline divider, so the bottom reads as one control surface. Reserved bottom padding drops 184dp → 156dp.

Screen 02

Player

The full-bleed cover stays — it is the theme's signature and the morph target, and changing it would mean re-authoring the mini→full transition. Everything below it collapses from seven bands to four, returning about 110dp to the artwork.

Changes
Cover
It dissolves instead of ending

The progressive blur begins inside the artwork's lower quarter rather than at its bottom edge, so there is no hard horizon between cover and controls. Height grows 288 → 330dp with the reclaimed space.

Band 1
Identity is three lines, no chip

Title, then author · narrator, then the chapter as a plain tinted line rather than a pill above the title. The whole line stays the tap target for the chapter list.

The series name moves off the top bar into the eyebrow slot where it belongs, and the top bar keeps only the collapse chevron and the overflow.

Band 2
One scrubber, two tracks, one time line

The chapter slider stays the thing you drag. Beneath it, a 2.5dp whole-book track with a tick per chapter and the current chapter's segment lit — position in the book becomes a picture, not a second control.

Replaces slider + time row + book bar + second time row. Times become one row: elapsed, book-remaining (dim, centred), chapter-remaining.

Band 3
Transport unchanged, ring optional

Same five controls at the same sizes. Optionally a thin accent ring around the play button carrying chapter progress, so it is glanceable with a thumb over the scrubber. See decision 04.

Band 4
Four loose icons become one rail

Skip-silence, audio settings, bookmarks and sleep timer sit in a single cover-glass pill with four slots, so they read as one utility set. Active states tint their own slot at accent 24% — same behaviour, contained.

The “Skip silence” text label is dropped; the slot tint carries the state, and the long-press sheet still explains it.

Unchanged
Return / Confirm / jump-restore pills

Transient, already correct, already ScrimPill. They keep their position between identity and scrubber.

Cover
Becomes the pill's left cap

64dp square, flush to the edge, clipped by the pill's own left round cap. The cover stops being a thumbnail inside a bar and becomes the bar's leading form — and since it is already the morph source, the transition starts at the size the eye is following.

Progress
Traces the pill — and you have already written it

A straight LinearProgressIndicator under a RoundedCornerShape(50%) is geometrically wrong: it stops short of both caps. Replace it with a stroke that walks the pill's outline.

WidgetPainter.drawPerimeterProgressBar already does exactly this with PathMeasure for RING / SQUARE / ROUNDED_SQUARE. Same recipe, Compose drawBehind instead of Canvas.

Glass
Keeps the cover's hue

GlassPillSurface's flat Color.Black @ 0.30 becomes lerp(Black, primary, 0.12) @ 0.34 applied as a bottom-weighted gradient rather than a sheet, so the smudge work it already does stops being neutralised. The same change fixes the nav pill.

Text
Accent-tinted like everything else

Title and author move from onSurface / onSurfaceVariant to ImmersiveStyle.scrimText(). This is currently the only Immersive surface whose text is not cover-tinted, and it is the most-seen one.

Subtitle
Author becomes chapter and time left

The author is on the cover you are looking at. “Chapter 9 · 41m left” is what you actually want from a collapsed player. Falls back to the author when a book has no chapters.

Approved 2026-08-19 — 1A 2A 3A 4B

Four calls, all four settled

Every recommendation was taken. The approved option in each pair is highlighted; the alternatives are kept on the record so a future change knows what was considered and rejected.

01 · Home hero

ASharp window into the backdrop. The blur resolves into focus at the top of the scroll and dissolves back as you scroll. approved
BDiscrete Continue card floating over the blur — closer to today's resume card. Safer, less distinctive.
CNo hero. Straight into the grid with the backdrop simply un-dimmed. Smallest change, keeps the most books above the fold.

02 · Mini player cover cap

AFlush square, clipped by the left cap. Morphs cleanly into the full player's square cover. approved
BCircular vinyl cap, optionally rotating while playing. Costs a corner-radius animation in the morph.

03 · Play button on grid cards

AKeep it, restyle as glass. No behaviour change, far quieter over the art. approved
BOnly on the now-playing card; elsewhere play moves into the long-press menu. Cleanest tiles, one extra tap for a common action.

04 · Progress ring on the play button

AYes — chapter progress stays readable with a thumb over the scrubber.
BNo. Band 2 already says it, twice is noise, and the ring complicates the morph's play-button target. approved

Found while reading, unrelated to the look

Three small things I would fix in the same pass

The cover morph starts at the wrong corner radius. BookGridCard clips at MaterialTheme.shapes.large = 24dp, but publishes ImmersiveHomeStyle.cardCornerRadius = 28dp to CoverBoundsRegistry. Every Book Info and player morph therefore begins 4dp rounder than the card it is growing out of. One-line fix, and the first frame lands exactly.

Mini bar text is not accent-tinted. Covered above — listed separately because it is a correctness gap against the theme's own stated rule (“all text accent-tinted”), not a design preference.

Immersive hard-codes two grid columns with the comment “Immersive has no landscape layout”. With a 250dp hero above it, landscape gets tight. Not fixing it in this pass — flagging that the hero should either become a side panel in landscape or stay portrait-only.
