Voyage · companion packs · shipped design

The companion stops
being a drawer.

Today it is a bottom sheet that covers the player, hides the transport, stacks a second sheet on itself to edit anything, and looks like two unrelated apps depending on the theme. This proposes one full-screen surface that keeps the audio controls — the player’s own controls, morphed into a dock — navigates from a single spine, gives every character a face, and edits in place.

01

Where it stands

Four problems, and they are all the same problem

The companion was built as a sheet because a sheet was the cheapest surface to reach from the player. Everything awkward about it follows from that one decision.

It covers the thing it describes

A ModalBottomSheet over the player. Open the companion and the transport is gone — so “who is this again?” costs you the controls, mid-listen, which is exactly when you asked.

Navigation is scattered across four surfaces

Header icons (map, edit, close), a “View map” text button, tap-the-rail, tap-a-bond — and editing opens a second modal sheet with its own dialogs on top. Four ways in, no sense of where you are.

The two themes are two different apps

Immersive got a crest-and-stat-line character sheet. Material You still renders identity:Rank as a literal row label. Same data, same ViewModel, unrecognisably different quality.

A cast with no faces

PackEntity.media has been in the pack format since day one. Nothing resolves it. Every character is a letter in a circle, so a cast of nine reads as a list of initials.

02

The shape

One surface, four bands

A persistent full-screen overlay drawn above the PlayerSheet — the pattern ChapterOverlay and SeriesOverlay already use, and the one §8 already specified for FULLSCREEN boards. Not a nav route: the player stays composed underneath, which is what makes the morph possible at all.

Band 1

Header — the pack’s name, an edit toggle, a close. Three controls, never more; everything that used to live here moved into the spine.

Band 2

The spine — one segmented control, four destinations: Cast · Map · Timeline · Pack. This is the single place navigation happens.

Band 3

The pane — whatever the spine selected. A character detail is not a fifth destination; it pushes inside the Cast pane and pops with back.

Band 4

The dock — the player’s transport, morphed down here and kept. Its top edge is the scrubber, carrying two marks: where you are, and how far the companion is revealed.

03

Drag it

The controls travel; they do not cross-fade

Opening the companion is one continuous move: the cover shrinks into the dock, the play button arcs down and right, the skip buttons close in behind it, the scrubber widens into the dock’s top edge, and the companion’s own chrome fades in underneath. Closing runs it backwards. Scrub it.

04

One place to navigate

The spine, and nothing else

Four destinations, always visible, always in the same place. Editing is not a fifth — it is a mode the whole surface enters, because you should change a thing where you are looking at it, not in a mirror-image list inside another sheet.

Back means one thing

Character → Cast → close. One predictive-back chain, no stacked modals to unwind and no “which sheet am I dismissing” ambiguity.

The badge has somewhere to land

The digest count on the player’s companion icon deep-links straight to Timeline, so “3 changes” answers itself instead of dropping you on a cast grid.

Edit is reversible and visible

One toggle. While it is on, every editable value grows an affordance in place and the header says so. Nothing is edited through a surface that hides the thing being edited.

05

Two themes, one skeleton

Same bands, different world

Bands, spine and dock are shared — one implementation, the way InfoPageScaffold shares Book Info and the Series page. Only the frame routes per theme: fills, shapes, ring vs. no ring, blur vs. elevation, pill vs. tab indicator. Change the deck once and both follow.

06

Faces

Every character has a portrait, and the fallback is not a failure

Three rungs, tried in order. The bottom rung is the crest that ships today — so a pack with no art still looks authored, and a pack with art looks like a cast.

What is missing today

PackEntity.media holds media/kelsier.webp and nothing reads it. Needed: CompanionDataStore.mediaFile(bookId, packId, rel) and its writer. Coil is already a dependency; the rest is an AsyncImage.

Seeding brings art

FandomSeedService already parses the article holding the infobox image. Pulling that file during a pregenerate is the difference between a seeded pack of initials and a seeded pack of faces — and it stays a draft you tick per character, like every other seeded fact.

Adding one by hand

Edit mode → tap a portrait → pick from gallery. The file is copied into the pack’s own media/ dir, so it travels with the export and survives a reinstall like everything else under the disk-is-truth rule.

07

Build

What this actually costs

One new shared scaffold, two thin frames, five panes, a choreography table and a media resolver. Two existing files dissolve rather than grow.

Unchanged on purpose

CompanionViewModel, EntityStateResolver, RevealDigest, RevealCursorRepository, the pack format and every unit test. This is a presentation change; the spoiler-proof-by-construction guarantee is untouched.

Suggested order

Deck scaffold + spine + panes on the existing data → portraits → the dock, still cross-fading → the morph last. Each step ships something better than what is there now.

Landscape

Two-column, as decided: header, spine and dock on a 176dp left rail, pane on the right. The header stacks its controls under the title there — beside two icon buttons in 176dp the title had about eight characters to work with.

What the build changed

Edit mode reads the author's unfiltered cast, not the revealed one. Anything else makes an author's own edit vanish the moment they make it, since the reveal cursor only advances by listening — which is precisely why the old editor had to be a separate surface, and the one thing that had to be solved before it could be deleted.
